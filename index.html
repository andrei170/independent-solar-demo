The warning signs

Six things that usually mean something is wrong.

If more than one of these sounds familiar, an assessment is probably worth the money. If none of them do, it probably is not, and we would rather tell you that now.

It generates less than you were told

The figure in the sales quote and the figure on the app do not match, and nobody has explained the gap.

The installer has gone

The company that fitted it has ceased trading, changed name, or simply will not return your calls.

Shading nobody mentioned

Panels sited where a chimney, a tree or a neighbouring building takes them out for part of the day.

The roof has suffered

Leaks, slipped tiles or flashing damage that appeared after the array went on.

Paperwork that never arrived

Missing certification, no handover pack, or a warranty you have never been able to produce.

Finance that does not add up

The repayment and the saving were presented as matching, and they do not.

Why independent matters here

Ask the company that fitted it and you are asking them to mark their own homework.

An installer assessing their own work

  • Has an obvious reason to find nothing wrong
  • May be the party you would be claiming against
  • Often quotes for more equipment as the answer
  • May no longer exist at all

An independent assessment

  • No relationship with whoever installed it
  • Nothing to sell you afterwards, because we do not fit systems
  • Written up as evidence, not as a sales document
  • Says so plainly when the install is actually fine

Including when the answer is that nothing is wrong

A fair number of systems we are asked to look at turn out to be performing as they should, and the problem is the expectation that was set during the sale. That is still a useful answer, and you get it in writing either way.

What gets checked

What an assessment actually covers.

Performance against design

  • Actual output against modelled yield
  • Inverter and monitoring data
  • Whether the original modelling was realistic

Installation quality

  • Mounting, fixings and roof penetrations
  • Cable management and isolation
  • Workmanship against standards

Documentation

  • Certification and handover pack
  • What was promised at point of sale
  • Warranty position, including if the installer has gone

Why this page asks ten questions when the current site asks none

Somebody who will answer ten questions about a system they are worried about is somebody with a real problem and a reason to pay to solve it. Somebody who will not, usually was not going to proceed. This is the filter, and it sits before the work rather than after the scaffolding.

Questions

The things people ask first.

Do you install solar?

No. That is the point. We have no installation business to protect and nothing to sell you at the end of the assessment.

What if the installer has gone out of business?

That is one of the more common reasons people come to us. The assessment still establishes what was done and what it is worth, which matters for a warranty, an insurer or a claim.

What does it cost?

A fixed fee, agreed in writing before anything begins. It depends on system size and whether a site visit is needed, which is why the form above asks what it does.

What if you find nothing wrong?

You get that in writing too. It happens, and it is still worth knowing.

Can I use the report in a dispute?

It is written as an evidenced document and it is yours. We are consultants rather than solicitors, so what you do with it is a decision to take with proper advice.

How long does it take?

Depends on whether we need to attend site and on how quickly monitoring data can be obtained. We will give you a timescale before you commit.
